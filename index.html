01 · PhD research

Mukha as āhārya interface.

Games, avatars, emoji, robots and mascots all need faces that carry character and emotion at a glance. Performance masks have done this job for centuries: readable to a crowd, at a distance, in lamp light, on a moving body. Yet almost nobody has studied how they are actually read.

The reframing

The common view is that masks express emotion the way faces do. My thesis argues that a mask is an āhārya device, part of a character's costume. It fixes who the character is and what they tend to feel. Performance completes the rasa.

Channel 1Identity · who is this?Colour, iconography, crown. Stable and rule-bound.
Channel 2Affective potential · what do they tend to feel?Form, proportion, exaggeration, outline.
↓ performance completes it: motion, light, music, the Sutradhara ↓

03 · Colour

Two colour systems.

The Nāṭyaśāstra gives colour two jobs. In one chapter each rasa, or aesthetic emotion, has a colour. In another, colour marks a character's type. Tap a rasa.

Fury

Raudra

Chapter VI

Colours of the rasas

Eight emotions, eight colours. Here colour describes a feeling: fury is red, wonder is yellow, fear is black.

Chapter XXIII

Āhārya colours

Characters are painted by habitation, birth and age. Demons are dark blue. Here colour describes who someone is, not what they feel.

Ravana is a furious king, yet his mask in Majuli is blue, not the red of raudra. So do the masks follow the emotion system, the character system, or a logic of their own? That is one of the questions this thesis tests.

04 · Fieldwork

Majuli, and the makers.

Majuli, a river island in Assam, holds the Neo-Vaishnavite Satras of Sankardeva's tradition. Masks are made for Bhaona, the performance of Ankiya Naat, with its gods, demons, animals and birds. In 2024 Majuli mask-making received a Geographical Indication tag.

The four Satras that make masks
SamaguriSatra
Natun SamaguriSatra
BihimpurSatra
Alengi NarasimhaSatra
MukhFace masks
LotokaiMid-size, with moving parts
Bor / cho mukhaLarge, full body
How the work is done

Consent from each Satradhikar and every artisan, in Assamese. Agreement in advance on what may be photographed or published. Credit and benefit-sharing for named makers. The principles are shared; the masks are not for copying.
